The engine is real; the business around it is not. Reservations, fleet, damage, deposits, counter and the AI agent all work end to end, and multi-tenancy now holds under adversarial testing.

What is missing is what turns that into something a rental company can buy and run on: they cannot be charged for the product, cannot bring their existing bookings in, cannot get their data out, cannot make the storefront look like theirs, and every quote under-collects tax.

Blockers

Nothing else should ship first. Each of these either loses money, breaks a legal obligation, or stops a customer becoming a customer.

Tax is zero on every quote pricingS

AvalaraClient is written and never instantiated, and the pricing service calls calculate_tax() — a method the class does not define, so even wiring it would fail. Under-collecting tax is an unrecoverable liability, not a workaround. For a one-to-three location operator the answer is a tax rate on the location, not a tax engine.

POST /pricing/quote → "taxes": "0.00", "total": "79.98"
Online bookings arrive unpaid paymentsM

The booking site's payment step is labelled “Demo mode” and card details never reach the API. An unpaid online reservation is a lead, not a booking, and a small operator has no collections function. Stripe pre-auth, capture and refund already work — only the storefront capture path is missing.

apps/web-booking/app/booking/[id]/page.tsx:697 → “Demo mode”
Counter check-out silently 404s operationsS

Three call sites POST to /api/v1/counter/*. No counter router is mounted, and the failure is deliberately swallowed with a comment saying the endpoints may not exist yet. The reservation, the vehicle and the money go out of sync with no error shown — unrecoverable on paper.

3 call sites → /api/v1/counter/… · routers mounted: 0
No rental agreement is ever produced legalshipped

Signature capture was real and hashed. The document was not — weasyprint was in requirements and imported nowhere. A signed agreement is legally required and cannot be recreated after the fact, which is exactly when it is needed.

GET /api/v1/checkout/agreements/{id}/document → PDF, rendered once and keyed Migration 068 stores the key so a later rate change cannot alter a signed document Migration 069 restores extras-name resolution for agreements predating 065
Forward bookings cannot be imported migrationL

An operator switching has roughly thirty live bookings and two hundred past customers. Vehicles import by CSV; nothing else does. So they run this and their spreadsheet in parallel, double-entering everything — and nobody sustains that past week two. This is the single highest-leverage missing feature for conversion, and it goes unbuilt everywhere because it is unglamorous.

import/export endpoints across all 22 domains: 1 (vehicles only)
No data export legalshipped

Not one export route existed. GDPR erasure is implemented properly, including legal holds — so the product could delete a person's data and could not give it to them. Export must also work while an account is past-due or suspended; holding a business's operating records over an unpaid invoice is not defensible.

GET /api/v1/tenants/me/export → zip of 24 CSVs + importer-shaped forward bookings Gated on workspace admin only, never on billing standing Isolation suite +4 probes (36/36); both new probes verified by mutation
No subscription billing commercialL

The billing domain is a twelve-line health check. stripe_customer_id and trial_ends_at exist on the tenant and nothing writes them. Plan limits are enforced for staff seats only — the one dimension that should not be billable — and the single upgrade prompt in the product is a sentence with no link, no price and nowhere to go. Self-serve signup with no way to charge is a free-tier generator.

Two async jobs write to S3 and there is no route to read a result. The Reports page is now hidden behind a flag rather than showing invented revenue. Most of the aggregation already exists in the dashboard domain — this is exposure and CSV export, not new computation.

Maintenance has no storage at all fleetM

No work-order table exists in any migration and the router is a stub. The genuine mechanism is a vehicle block with type MAINTENANCE, plus automatic recall holds from the daily VIN scan. Minimum viable is a work order attached to that block — one table, not a subsystem.

Counter offline queue never drains operationsM

An offline checkout is queued to local storage and the operator is told it will sync. Nothing ever sends it, and the drain functions are called from nowhere. Revenue-bearing data loss, reported to the user as success.

Onboarding does not survive contact with a new operator activationM

Two competing definitions of “ready” exist. The checklist opens at 60% with three items auto-satisfied, and its staff step can never be completed by a solo operator. Unverified workspaces are hard-deleted after fourteen days against a verification token that lives twenty-four hours — with no warning email. Someone who misses the email and returns in three weeks finds their workspace gone.

Later

Real, but deferrable. Two entries here are deliberately not scheduled — they imply capability the product does not have.

Custom domains white-labelL

Will be the loudest request and is correctly deferred. A verified acme-rcm.ceez.ai carrying the operator's own logo, colours, copy and legal text is most of the perceived value at a fraction of the cost and none of the certificate support burden. The on-demand TLS mechanism built this week already serves customer domains unchanged when the time comes.

Corporate accounts salesM

No table exists. What a small operator actually needs is “invoice the panel shop monthly” — a customer flag and an invoice, not an account hierarchy with credit limits. The hidden fabricated page is not evidence of demand.

Weekend rates never apply pricingS

WEEKEND_SPECIAL is public but ranked below the rack rate on purpose: nothing evaluates whether the rental falls on a weekend, so promoting it would silently discount midweek hires. Inert until that date logic exists — the safer of two wrong behaviours, and a deliberate choice rather than an oversight.

Dead integrations that imply they work cleanupS

Booking.com and Expedia connectors raise NotImplementedError on every method while a five-minute job polls them and logs success. A toll task is scheduled against a task name that does not exist. The Tyro gateway is a skeleton. Remove the schedule entries and stubs so the codebase stops claiming capability it lacks.
